## Question 3.1

What is the entire training objective of GPT, Claude, LLaMA, and every modern LLM? Why is this surprising?

---

**Answer:**

[*] The training objective is: given a sequence of words, predict the next word. Nothing more.
[*] It is surprising because from this single simple task — at massive scale — LLMs learn grammar, facts, logic, code, style, math, and much more.
[*] To predict the next word perfectly in every context, the model must implicitly learn: grammar (syntactically correct sentences), facts (geography, history), logic (if A → B), code (algorithms), style (literary voices), and math.
[*] Shannon's 1951 experiment showed that prediction ability and language comprehension are deeply linked — LLMs scaled this insight up from letter-guessing to billion-parameter models.
[ ] LLMs are trained with explicit grammar rules and a handcrafted knowledge base.
[ ] LLMs are trained by having humans rate their outputs (this describes RLHF fine-tuning, not pre-training).

## Question 3.2

Fill in the GPT scaling table: what was the key surprise at each model generation?

---

**Answer:**

[*] GPT-1 (2018, 117M params): Pre-training + fine-tuning works across many tasks the model was not designed for.
[*] GPT-2 (2019, 1.5B params): Generates coherent multi-paragraph text. OpenAI refused to release the full model, calling it "too dangerous."
[*] GPT-3 (2020, 175B params): Can perform tasks it was never trained for — few-shot learning emerges.
[*] GPT-4 (2023, ~1.8T rumored): Passes the bar exam, writes production code.
[*] Pattern: each step was a bet — "what if we just make it bigger?" — and each time the model surprised even its creators.
[ ] GPT-2 was released immediately without any safety review.
[ ] GPT-3's capabilities were fully predicted before training began.

## Question 3.3

What are "emergent abilities" in LLMs? Give two examples.

---

**Answer:**

[*] Emergent abilities: capabilities that appear suddenly at certain model scales — they are completely absent in smaller models and then appear seemingly out of nowhere as the model grows.
[*] Example 1: Multi-step arithmetic (appears at ~100B params) — small models get multi-digit multiplication wrong; large models get it right.
[*] Example 2: Chain-of-thought reasoning (appears at ~100B params) — only large models can reason step-by-step when prompted.
[*] Other examples: code generation (~10B+), analogy making (~50B+), zero-shot translation (~50B+), theory of mind (~100B+).
[ ] Emergent abilities are capabilities explicitly programmed into the model at a certain scale.
[ ] All LLM capabilities appear gradually and smoothly — there are no sudden jumps.

## Question 3.4

What is the "chain-of-thought scratchpad theory"? Why does asking the model to "think step by step" improve accuracy?

---

**Answer:**

[*] An LLM's output is its scratchpad — it reasons by writing. Each generated token becomes part of the context that helps generate the next token.
[*] The Transformer performs a fixed amount of computation per token. When the model generates intermediate reasoning steps, harder problems produce longer chains, effectively giving the model more compute.
[*] Without scratchpad: Question → Answer (one big jump, often wrong). With scratchpad: Question → Step 1 → Step 2 → Step 3 → Answer (each step adds context for the next).
[*] Example: without CoT, "15 apples − 6 + 10" gives wrong answer; with CoT step-by-step, gives correct 19.
[ ] Chain-of-thought works because it simplifies the question into smaller sub-questions automatically.
[ ] Asking the model to think step by step causes it to access a separate reasoning module.

## Question 3.5

What are the two axes of LLM improvement, and what event marked the second axis?

---

**Answer:**

[*] Axis 1: Training-time compute — make the model bigger/better (more parameters, more data). Example: GPT-3 → GPT-4.
[*] Axis 2: Inference-time compute — let the model think longer at answer time (chain-of-thought, self-correction). Example: GPT-4 → o1.
[*] The second axis was marked by OpenAI releasing o1 (September 2024, codename "Strawberry") — a model that uses chain-of-thought internally before producing its final answer.
[*] On AIME math: GPT-4 scored 13%; o1 scored 83%. On Codeforces programming: o1 reached the 89th percentile.
[ ] The second axis of improvement is increasing the training data size.
[ ] The two axes are: supervised learning and unsupervised learning.

## Question 3.6

What is the "inference-time scaling" insight? How does it change how we think about model quality?

---

**Answer:**

[*] Inference-time scaling: you can trade compute for accuracy at answer time — giving the model more time to think produces better answers.
[*] Strategies: generate 1 chain (baseline), generate 10 chains and pick the best (significantly better), generate 100 chains and verify each (even better), let the model self-correct and retry (better still).
[*] The same model can give different quality answers depending on how much compute you allow at inference.
[*] Analogy: Axis 1 is like studying more for an exam (learning); Axis 2 is like taking more time on each exam question (thinking).
[ ] Inference-time scaling means running more GPUs in parallel to get faster (not better) answers.
[ ] A fixed compute budget per token means all questions get equal reasoning effort — this is a feature, not a limitation.

## Question 3.7

What is the "Stochastic Parrot" argument? What is the strongest counter-evidence?

---

**Answer:**

[*] The Stochastic Parrot argument (Bender & Gebru, 2021): LLMs are "haphazardly stitching together sequences of linguistic forms without any reference to meaning" — like a parrot mimicking speech without understanding it.
[*] Supporting evidence: LLMs fail when math problems are slightly rephrased; they hallucinate confidently; they have no world model; answers change with rephrasing.
[*] Counter-evidence: LLMs make novel combinations never seen in training data (more than memorization); they transfer across domains (text → math, code, medicine); researchers found LLMs build linear internal representations of truth.
[*] Honest answer: LLMs don't reason like humans, but they do something more than memorization — the exact nature is not yet fully understood.
[ ] The Stochastic Parrot paper concluded that LLMs are truly intelligent.
[ ] All AI researchers agree that LLMs are just autocomplete and have no internal representations.

## Question 3.8

What is the "World Model Hypothesis"? What evidence supports it?

---

**Answer:**

[*] Hypothesis: LLMs build compressed implicit world models inside their neural networks — because to predict text accurately across all domains, the model must learn something about how the world actually works.
[*] Evidence: LLMs correctly answer spatial reasoning questions, causal reasoning questions, and social/emotional questions that require understanding physics, causality, and human behavior — all learned only from text.
[*] The argument: to predict text about physics → must learn a model of physics; about code → must learn a model of computation; about people → must learn a model of human behavior.
[*] These world models are imperfect and implicit but real enough to produce correct answers in many cases.
[ ] The World Model Hypothesis claims LLMs have full, explicit simulations of physics and biology.
[ ] LLMs answer spatial questions by looking them up in a database, not by modeling the world.
